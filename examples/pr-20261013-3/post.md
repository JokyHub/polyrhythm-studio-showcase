Orchestre Poly-Rythmo de Cotonou made The 1st Album twice. In 1973 the band from Cotonou, Dahomey (now Benin), went to Lagos for its better studios to record for the Albarika Store label. Albarika rejected the first take for background noise, so they cut it again.
---
Bandleader Mélomé Clément formed the group in Cotonou in the late 1960s. Before the sessions it lured singer Vincent Ahéhéhinnou from rival band Daho Jazz; he wrote all four songs. Critics hear Dahomey's vodun rhythms alongside highlife, Afro-Cuban jazz, James Brown and Fela.
---
Abroad, the band's hundreds of recordings came back through reissues: Soundway's The Kings of Benin in 2004, then Analog Africa anthologies from 2008. In 2009 it played outside Africa for the first time. Strut released its new album Cotonou Club in 2011.
---
Also in 2011, Analog Africa reissued The 1st Album with two tracks from the rejected session, released there for the first time. Clément died in December 2012, aged 67; the band carried on with Madjafalao (Because Music, 2016).
---
Start with "Ou C'est Lui Ou C'est Moi", the album's opener. To my ear, follow the drums rolling under the organ's stabs and drones: the tension builds through repetition, not chord changes. Then hear the band decades later on Cotonou Club.
