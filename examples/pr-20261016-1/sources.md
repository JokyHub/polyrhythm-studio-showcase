## Conflicts / not verified
- Ilori's composer credits (other than "Prayer", whose title names him) rest on Wikipedia only; Discogs and AllMusic returned 403. Safer wording: "Ilori, who leads several of its pieces" or cite "credited on the album page"; or state only that the album opens with his "Prayer".
- Blakey's West Africa stay: Wikipedia says he travelled "for a year in West Africa, in 1948" but quotes him saying "I stayed two years"; EBSCO says "nearly two years"; Pan African Music says "a decisive year travelling in West Africa in 1948". Do not state a duration; "in the late 1940s" or "after his 1948 trip to West Africa" is safe.
- Ilori biography: Wikipedia says born c. 1934 to Nigerian parents in Nsawam, Gold Coast (Ghana), moved to the US in 1958 (weak citations: blog, All About Jazz review). Pan African Music only calls him Nigerian. NOT VERIFIED: do not state birthplace or birth year. "Nigerian singer and percussionist" is supported by pan-african-music.com.
- Track spellings vary ("Ayiko Ayiko" vs "Akiyo akiyo"; "Love, The Mystery of" vs "The Mystery of Love"). Use the Wikipedia/jazzdisco spelling.
- Yusef Lateef's extra instrument: "cow horn" (Wikipedia, Pan African Music) vs "cowbell" (jazzdisco). Omit it or say "flute, oboe and tenor".
- Guy Warren is in the log (2026-10-12): composer credit only, not featured.

## Listening angle (opinion)
- "Ife L'ayo (There Is Happiness in Love)": to my ear, the joy is the opening, where Ilori's penny whistle and Lateef's flute share a folk-like melody over the drums, then listen for how Blakey drops back into the ensemble as one drummer among many rather than the leader out front.
