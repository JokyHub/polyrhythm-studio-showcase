Subject: Bill Withers — "Use Me" (Still Bill, Sussex Records, 1972). Credit spotlight: James Gadson (drums, percussion), with Melvin Dunlap (bass guitar) and Ray Jackson (clavinet). Recorded at Record Plant, Los Angeles.

- "Use Me" was written by Bill Withers and is side one, track 4 (3:45) of Still Bill, which was released on Sussex Records on 1 May 1972; side one and side two together make up tracks 1–10 on CD. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Still Bill was recorded at Record Plant, Los Angeles, California. Second source: the credits transcription lists "Recorded At Record Plant, LA". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill ; https://sessiondays.com/2023/06/17/1972-bill-withers-still-bill/ (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Track personnel for "Use Me": Bill Withers (lead vocals, acoustic guitar), Ray Jackson (clavinet), Melvin Dunlap (bass guitar), James Gadson (drums and percussion). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_Me_(Bill_Withers_song) (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Album personnel matches this: Gadson on drums and percussion, Dunlap on bass, Jackson on piano/clavinet/Wurlitzer and horn and string arrangements, and Benorce Blackmon on guitar. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill ; https://sessiondays.com/2023/06/17/1972-bill-withers-still-bill/ (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Independent press confirmation that Gadson is the drummer on "Use Me": Complex's obituary says he "provided the beat on classic soul material like Ross' 'Love Hangover,' Withers' 'Use Me'". — https://www.complex.com/music/a/jaelaniturnerwilliams/james-gadson-legendary-drummer-dies (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Dunlap's electric bass tone is described as "best exemplified" on Withers tracks such as "Use Me" and "Kissing My Love" (Wikipedia cites Bass Player, 2013). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Dunlap (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Production credits on Still Bill (1972): Bill Withers and Ray Jackson are credited as producers on tracks 1–12, and Benorce Blackmon, Melvin Dunlap and James Gadson on tracks 1–10. "Use Me" is track 4, so all five are credited producers on it. The second source lists the same five producers, without track ranges: "Benorce Blackmon, Bill Withers, James Gadson, Melvin Dunlap, Ray Jackson". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_Bill ; https://sessiondays.com/2023/06/17/1972-bill-withers-still-bill/ (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Gadson played with Charles Wright & the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band. Two independent sources: Wikipedia says he "played with the first line-up" and recorded three albums with them between 1968 and 1970 (cites AllMusic), and Complex's obituary says "Gadson played in the Charles Wright & the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band". — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gadson ; https://www.complex.com/music/a/jaelaniturnerwilliams/james-gadson-legendary-drummer-dies (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- Dunlap was also a member of the Watts band and joined Withers's band with other Watts band members (Wikipedia cites a 2019 NAMM oral-history interview). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Dunlap (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- The single "Use Me" (Sussex), with "Let Me in Your Life" on the B-side, peaked at No. 2 on both the Billboard Hot 100 and the Hot Soul Singles chart. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Use_Me_(Bill_Withers_song) (retrieved 2026-10-12)
- James Gadson (born Kansas City, Missouri, 17 June 1939) died on 2 April 2026 at age 86. Refer to him in the past tense. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gadson ; https://www.legacy.com/person/James-Gadson-61092075 (retrieved 2026-10-12)

## Conflicts / not verified
- Single release month: the infobox on the "Use Me" Wikipedia page says August 1972, but its Reception section says September 1972 in the US. Not resolved (45cat and Billboard could not be fetched). Write "1972" only.
- Gadson's age at death: Complex's headline says 86 but its body says 87. Wikipedia and Legacy.com say 86. Complex dates the announcement to 3 April, while Wikipedia and Legacy give 2 April as the date of death. Leave the death out of the post or use "died in April 2026".
- Length: 3:45 on Wikipedia, 3:49 on the Sessiondays/Discogs-style listing. Do not state a length.
- Producer track ranges (1–10 vs 1–12) come from Wikipedia only. The second source confirms all five producers but gives no track ranges. Because tracks 11–12 are 2003 bonus live tracks, the claim that all five produced "Use Me" (track 4) holds on both sources.
- The Still Bill Wikipedia lead ("musicians from the Watts 103rd Street Rhythm Band") has no inline citation and names nobody. Use only the per-musician claims above: Gadson (two sources) and Dunlap (Wikipedia/NAMM only, so it is a single source and should be framed carefully or left out). Ray Jackson's Watts membership was NOT verified. Do not call him a Watts band member.
- Sessiondays transcribes Jackson's credit as "Clarinet". This is almost certainly a mistranscription of clavinet. The two Wikipedia pages (album and song) both say clavinet, so use "clavinet".

## Listening angle (opinion)
- "Use Me": to my ear, the trick is how little Gadson plays. The kick and snare stay dry and close, almost conversational, and the space he leaves lets Jackson's clipped clavinet and Dunlap's round, sliding bass lines pull against each other while Withers's vocal sits on top. Listen for how the clavinet and bass answer each other between the vocal lines, and how the groove never gets louder, only tighter. (Opinion. Make no technical claims about the miking or the drum kit.)
